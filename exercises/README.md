# Exercises — Penn Epigenetics Institute, Thursday 24 September 2026

Three exercises, done during the taught session. Each one takes about half an hour
and produces something you keep.

| | Exercise | You'll learn | You'll have |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | [First contact](exercise-1-first-contact.md) | Agents act on files; the plan–act–check loop; how to interrogate a result | `LEDGER.md` |
| 2 | [Teach the agent your project](exercise-2-teach-the-agent.md) | Context that persists between sessions, and how to tell whether it helped | A context file |
| 3 | [Write one skill](exercise-3-write-a-skill.md) | Procedures written once, loaded on demand; why the description is the whole game | A skill |

## Bring your own material, or use ours

Every exercise works two ways. **Use your own material if you have something safe** — a
public dataset, a folder of papers, an analysis repo, a talk outline. You'll learn more
from a real task you actually care about, and the point of the day is that these tools
work on your material rather than on a teaching example.

If you don't have anything to hand, or your own thing turns out to be too big, each
exercise names a fallback from the workshop repository. Use it without hesitation —
that's what it's for.

## The one rule

Whenever the agent produces a result, ask it:

> How do I know this is right? Show me your checks.

Then write down what you actually verified. That's what the ledger is for, and it is the
one habit worth carrying out of the room.

## Privacy

No PHI. No unpublished data you haven't cleared. No grant drafts. If you're unsure whether
something is safe to put in, that uncertainty is your answer — use the fallback material
instead and ask during the session.
